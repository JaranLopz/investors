What I look for

A great prototype doesn’t guarantee delivery

Most funding risk in game development sits in three places.

Scope vs. budget

Is the planned content achievable with the money and time requested, or does it assume a team that doesn’t yet exist?

Money model

Does the P&L handle platform fees, taxes, publisher splits and recoupment in the right order, with realistic sales assumptions?

Delivery capacity

Does anyone on the team have experience shipping at this size, and is there a credible production plan behind the timeline?

How the review works

Three steps, from brief to decision

  1. 1

    Confidential brief

    You share the pitch deck, roadmap and P&L under NDA. If the studio can’t share something, we note the gap rather than guess.

  2. 2

    Independent review

    I test the scope, financial model and delivery plan against what comparable projects have needed. I can join one call with the studio to clarify key points.

  3. 3

    Written memo

    A short document with red, amber and green findings, the questions to put to the team, and the fixes I would prioritise. The decision stays with you.

How I work

Clear boundaries, so the review stays useful

  • Independent: You pay me directly, not the studio. I don’t take equity, a finder’s fee or any payment linked to the deal closing.
  • Production focus: I assess delivery and financial plausibility. I don’t judge the game’s market potential or give investment advice.
  • No guarantees: A review reduces risk; it doesn’t remove it or predict returns.
  • Not a substitute for legal, tax or financial due diligence. Your own advisers should cover those areas.
